# Financial/GST review and invoice-issuance hold

**Status: local review only; real invoicing is blocked.** No hosted migration, production deployment, external tax consultation, provider integration or financial-data access occurred. The existing production Supabase project was previously found to have no app schema/migrations, and has not been rechecked or changed during this step. A legal/tax review and real-provider security validation remain required. This document is not tax advice and passing arithmetic tests is not a GST-compliance certification.

## What the local code currently does

- Invoice and item amounts are stored as integer paise; SQL calculations use PostgreSQL `numeric` and explicit bounds. A line's gross amount is `round(quantity × unit_price_paise)`; taxable = gross − discount; total tax = `round(taxable × gst_rate_bps / 10000)`; line total = taxable + CGST + SGST + IGST. For positive values PostgreSQL's numeric round rounds half away from zero. Invoice header and line sums are checked at issuance. Shipping, extra charges and round-off are header amounts. These are implementation rules, not conclusions about required legal rounding.
- The existing `issue_invoice` checks active owner/admin/manager membership, locks the invoice, allows an existing-issued replay, checks lines/totals and tax-distribution consistency, and checks business registration and state; a separate trigger requires a GSTIN for GST issuance. FK/RLS scope business and customer. It does **not** independently establish the applicable tax rate or product/service classification.
- A synthetic local 105-paise taxable line at 18% produces 19 paise total tax: intra-state example 10/9 paise CGST/SGST or inter-state example 19 paise IGST; header total 124 paise. A 19/0 intra-state split is rejected on issue. These are arithmetic test vectors only, not a statement that either split/rate is legally correct for an actual supply.
- The UI currently creates **non-GST draft only**, using new candidate migration 008. The 008 transaction makes invoice, number, lines, audit and idempotency record atomic; repeated identical key/payload replays and different payload is rejected. It does not implement GST, shipping/discounts or invoice issue/send. Its request key is persisted only within the same browser tab/session: a new key or different tab cannot guarantee deduplication.
- A `proof_attached` flag is not malware verification. Payment-proof attach, upload, download and preview remain held. Existing proof-hold candidate migration 007 is not yet applied to hosted DB.

## Local candidate 009: deny new issuance

`supabase/migrations/20261107000900_hold_invoice_issuance.sql` adds a BEFORE INSERT/UPDATE trigger on `public.invoices`, rejecting a new row already marked issued or a transition from another state to issued. Thus the pre-existing `SECURITY DEFINER public.issue_invoice` cannot issue a draft through direct SQL/RPC while the trigger is active. Existing issued rows and identical `issue_invoice` replays are not modified; context-unchanged operations, historical no-proof payments and voids are retained. Normal privileged inserts/updates are also denied, but table owners/superusers can disable/replace triggers or bypass ordinary trigger execution. The guard is **not deployed** and does not protect a hosted project until separately applied and verified; it is not a complete ledger-immutability control. Do not remove it simply to unblock the UI.

## Review findings and release blockers

1. **Tax determination is unresolved.** Product/service classification, effective rates and exemptions, HSN/SAC and invoice disclosure, place-of-supply, interstate/intrastate/UT and other special cases require an Indian GST professional and authoritative current rules. Current SQL checks a state relationship and mathematical distribution only. It accepts rate values 0–10000 basis points and nullable HSN/SAC; acceptance does not mean the selected rate/classification is correct or incorrect for a particular supply.
2. **Rounding policy is not approved for every tax scenario.** The code rounds per line and accepts intra-state CGST/SGST difference up to one paise. Item/header rounding, fractional quantities, split-tax allocation, discounts, shipping/charges, invoice-level rounding and returns/credit notes must be checked against applicable rules and professional advice.
3. **Existing-invoice immutability is not fully established against privileged writers.** Authenticated update policies limit edits to draft, but database administrators/definers and ownership powers are outside ordinary RLS; catalog and other writers require review. Migration 009 prevents new issuance, not arbitrary alteration of existing financial history by privileged roles.
4. **Invoice identity and customer data.** FY numbering is allocated when a draft is saved and gaps are allowed. Whether a draft number should be reserved at that stage, cancellation and legal numbering/retention, customer address/tax ID snapshots and later customer changes need explicit product/tax decisions. The current draft form omits many commercial/tax fields.
5. **Financial workflow is incomplete.** No UI for issue/send, cancellation, payment creation, credit notes, GST draft or safe draft editing. UI absence is not a security control; `public.issue_invoice` is callable by granted roles before 009 is applied. Existing `record_payment` remains behind the separate proof guard for non-NULL proof only once 007 is actually installed.
6. **Real-provider and migration blockers.** The Supabase project has no app migrations yet; current connector's migration version behavior does not preserve local timestamps. Do not apply an unreviewed series that temporarily exposes issue/proof paths or leaves migration history divergent. Migration 009 must be applied atomically with any initial schema exposure, after a verified plan, and its post-install triggers/grants tested. Do not claim local mock tests prove hosted Auth, PostgREST or Postgres behavior.
7. **Operational safeguards.** Real idempotency/retry, concurrency, immutable audit/retention, privilege separation, migration rollback, restore/reconciliation, authorization and accuracy under failure require independent review and real-provider tests. An outage must not be represented as zero invoices or a successful financial write.

## Evidence from local synthetic tests

- Two GST arithmetic tests above run against the unmodified baseline issuance logic before installing 009. Both run in transactions that roll back. They prove only the specified synthetic SQL behavior.
- Three local guard checks after 009: direct `issue_invoice` and normal privileged insert/update attempts reject with no invoice/audit change; pre-existing issued replay and no-proof payment/void preserve history; runtime cannot execute/disable the trigger function. A fresh-schema upgrade scenario creates a historical issued row before applying the hold, then confirms a new draft cannot be issued. The role/actor environment is mock Supabase auth on disposable local PostgreSQL, not PostgREST or real hosted Supabase.
- Latest isolated suite: **55 ordinary mock-Supabase checks passed, four separate baseline unscanned-proof vulnerabilities demonstrated** (security acceptance FAIL); Neon lab 44 ordinary checks plus one known proof gap; storage 40, Better Auth 29, integration 39 and lab upgrade 9 passed. Test process exit 0 means expected local outcomes were observed; it is not authorization to release. The PostgreSQL service was stopped, listeners checked and jail removed.

## Conditions before lifting hold

1. Agree with an Indian tax professional on supported invoice types, rate/classification lookup, fields and legal requirements, rounding and numbering; obtain representative accepted/rejected vectors.
2. Implement and independently review safe draft editing and issuance, financial arithmetic, tax decisions and audit/immutability; include direct RPC, role and concurrency tests rather than UI-only protection.
3. Verify real Supabase grants/RLS, PostgREST exposure, no-proof payment, proof deny, historical records, deployment order, migration history, failure/rollback and backups in an approved isolated provider environment.
4. Keep proof and recovery holds. Obtain explicit authorization to apply migrations and separately release/enable financial flows. Never silently change a posted invoice or ledger during rollback.
